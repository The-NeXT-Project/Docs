# Support and Audit

## Tickets

**Support** → **Tickets** is a private conversation with the operator. Open one with a type — Usage, Billing, Account or Other — a subject, and a description of the problem.

Tickets support Markdown, so a configuration snippet in a code fence stays readable instead of being mangled.

Practical notes:

- Most sites cap how many tickets you may open in a calendar month. The quota resets on the first, not on a rolling thirty days.
- A closed ticket takes no more replies. Open a new one if the problem comes back.
- Replies arrive by email, or through your linked IM account if you set one as your preferred contact method.
- An operator can edit or delete their own replies. An edit is stamped with a timestamp you can see.

### What to include

The operator can see your account, your orders and your logs — they do not need you to quote your traffic figures back at them. What they cannot see is what you did and what happened, so include:

- what you tried, and on which client and platform;
- which node, if it is one node;
- when it started;
- the exact error, rather than a description of it.

### Replies from an assistant

Some sites use a language model to draft or send replies, signed with a name like *AI Assistant* rather than a person's. It can look up your account, your orders and the site's documentation, so its answers are usually specific rather than generic. If the answer misses the point, say so in the same ticket — a person reads the thread.

## Announcements

**Support** → **Announcements** is what the operator is telling everyone: outages, new nodes, price changes, maintenance windows. Pinned announcements stay at the top and also appear on your dashboard. Some are emailed as well.

Worth reading before opening a ticket about something being down.

## Documentation

**Support** → **Documentation** is the operator's own guides, specific to their site — which client they recommend, how to configure it, what their rules are. Some sites show this section to paying users only.

## Audit

If the site shows them, **Support** → **Audit** has two pages.

**Rules** lists what traffic is matched against, each with a description of what it is for. Sites publish these so you can avoid tripping them, which is a good reason to read them.

**Logs** lists your own matches — which rule, which node, when.

### If you are banned by the audit system

Repeated matches can ban an account automatically. The ban is time-limited and lifts on its own; the site's ban page tells you the reason. A ban set by hand by an operator carries their own explanation instead, and does not lift automatically — that one needs a ticket.
